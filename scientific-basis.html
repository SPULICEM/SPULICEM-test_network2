Scientific basis

Scientific basis

As with any interdisciplinary project in environmental science, our work combines several theoretical frameworks and connects them to real-world applications. The sections below outline the building blocks of our approach.

Policy Coherence Literature

The policy coherence literature grows out of policy process studies. It provides theoretical and empirical explanations of the extent to which policy can be shaped to align with higher-order objectives. We examine the coherence of objectives, strategies, and instruments (Cairney, 2025; Dzebo et al., 2025).

Concept box

What Is Coherence?

We use the definition from Bocquillon (2018), which describes coherence as a process through which a policy image is created. This process binds different policy objectives and instruments across traditional government sectors, guided by a set of directing principles that a wide range of actors perceive as legitimate.

Ideas are one way policy processes take shape. Recent work in the policy coherence area treats ideas, interests, and institutions as the backbone of the policy process that produces coherence or incoherence (Shawoo et al., 2023).

Ideas, Beliefs, and the Advocacy Coalition Framework

Because our work focuses on ideas and beliefs, we draw on the Advocacy Coalition Framework (ACF), a well-established approach in political science. The ACF explains how ideas and beliefs shape actors and coalitions and inform the policy process (Weible et al., 2011, 2020). Our definitions draw on types of beliefs and how they interact within actors’ disputes (Weible et al., 2020). Work on cross-coalition coordination further illustrates how coalitions form and cooperate (Aerne & Bonoli, 2023).

Network Perspective and Discourse Networks

We adopt a network perspective, specifically the discourse network approach (Leifeld, 2017). This approach combines discourse affinity, which binds actors together. Discourses represent the intentions behind collective action and how problems and solutions are framed. For this reason, the approach is central to providing quantitative measures for the policy coherence discussion.

Research Questions and Hypotheses

Main research question

How is the governance of smart, resilient, and sustainable cities in São Paulo, Santos, and São José dos Campos, from a discursive networked perspective?

Secondary research question

What might be the potential pathways to improve policy coherence of smart, resilient, and sustainable cities?

Hypotheses

  • Uniting actors who belong to the same discursive coalition will improve coherence (Weible et al., 2020; Kurze & Lenschow, 2018).
  • Disclosing which beliefs are more stable and which are more contested will improve coherence (Skovgaard, 2018).
  • Identifying brokers who can mediate multiple interests across groups will improve coherence (citation pending; Bocquillon, 2018).
  • Providing a more accurate measure of belief coherence potential will reveal the limits to coherence (Kefeli et al., 2023; Gerstenberg et al., 2026).

Building on this theoretical approach, we apply it to the role of ideas and beliefs in research agendas on smart cities, resilient cities, and decarbonised cities.
