Short introduction

It is always a good idea to increase coherence of our policies! However, it is not as easy as the books say. How can we improve our coherence in our governance systems?

This is the main question that policy studies have pursued in the past decades. There are no formulas to be applied. Each case is special.

In this project, we aim to explore governance arrangements from a network science perspective.

Why?

Because daily activity is shaped not only by norms, resources, and commands, but also by collaboration between institutions and people. From this network perspective on governance, we aim to offer a relational view: the more relations we have, the greater our potential to collaborate.

How do we do this?

We use discourse analysis techniques to identify the main beliefs that institutions sustain. The proposition is: if both institutions sustain similar beliefs, then they have more potential to collaborate. We call these belief affinities, which have been seen as a relevant driver to sustain or provide new collaboration, to shape new policy design, or to explain disagreements about how to implement solutions.

“Finding new groups that think similarly may surprise you with who you can collaborate with, because they sustain the same beliefs.”

Everybody wants to improve social and environmental well-being, but from different perspectives. There is always common ground for collaboration.

The question we explore

What communities, actors, and beliefs may inform policy integration in the smart, resilient, and sustainable city agendas in São Paulo, Santos, and São José dos Campos?
